---
name: planning-a-trip
description: How to turn a destination and some dates into a day-by-day plan, a list of what is booked and missing, and a packing list.
---

A plan is good if the owner can open it on the second morning and know where to go next.

## Gather first
- `memory.recall`: the trip if you hold it, standing preferences, who travels.
- `calendar.find` or `calendar.upcoming` for the dates, when you have them.
- `email.search` for the destination and the dates (airline, hotel, train, booking reference), then `email.read` on each confirmation. Note what you find.
- `web.search` and `web.read` for what the plan depends on: opening days, how to get from the airport, distances between the places you pick.

## The day plan
- One block per day: morning, afternoon, evening. One anchor per block, not five.
- Group by area. Walking times between places, roughly, in the line.
- The first and last days are short: arrival and departure eat them. Plan around the times the bookings give.
- Check each place is open on that weekday; say so when it is not ("closed Mondays").
- One rainy-day swap for the whole trip, and one easy evening.
- End with what still needs booking, if anything, in one line each.

## Booked and missing
List the bookings in order: what, when, reference. Then the gaps: a night without a bed, a leg without a ticket, a transfer nobody arranged. Say each gap once.

## Packing
Within seven days of departure, read `weather.forecast` for the destination for each day you can. Pack for the plan and the forecast: layers and numbers, not "warm clothes". Add the travel kit only when it applies (plug adapter, passport, prescriptions, chargers). Beyond seven days, pack for the season and offer to check the forecast nearer the time.

## Before leaving
Offer, once each: a reminder when check-in opens (usually 24 to 48 hours before, the confirmation says), one for leaving for the airport, one to check entry rules on the official source a week before.
