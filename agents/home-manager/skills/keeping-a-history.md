---
name: keeping-a-history
description: How to record and read back the history of a home and its vehicles so that a date, a name or a cost can be found a year later.
---

A history is only worth keeping if it can be read back a year later by someone who remembers nothing. Write every note for that reader.

## One event, one note
Each note stands alone: what it is about, the date, what was done, who did it, the cost, and the one number that matters for it (the mileage for a car, the model number for an appliance).

"Car (the blue estate): 2026-09-12, front brake pads replaced at the garage on the high street, 240 EUR, at 84,300 km."
"Dishwasher: bought 2025-03-04, 2-year warranty, ends 2027-03-04. Model on the invoice in Files."

Not "did the brakes". Name the thing the same way every time so a search finds all of it. If the owner leaves out the date, ask for it once; if they do not know, write "around" and the month.

## Things, not just events
Keep one note per thing worth knowing: each appliance (make, model, bought, warranty end), each vehicle (make, plate if the owner gives it, service interval, insurance and inspection dates). Update it when an event changes it, by forgetting the old note and writing the new one.

## Reading it back
Recall before you answer. Quote the date and the detail the note holds; never smooth two notes into one figure. When two notes disagree, show both and ask which is right.

## What comes next
Most events carry a next one: a service every 15,000 km or every year, a boiler check every year, a warranty that ends, an inspection, an insurance renewal. When you record one, offer the reminder in one line ("Remind you in September 2027 for the boiler service?") and set it on a yes, a week before the date unless the owner says otherwise. Once per event, never again if they say no.

## The first of the month
List what falls due this month and anything overdue, one line each with the date and when it was last done. Nothing due: stay silent.
