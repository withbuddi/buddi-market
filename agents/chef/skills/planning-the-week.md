---
name: planning-the-week
description: How to plan a week of dinners the household will actually cook, and the shopping list that goes with it.
---

A plan is good if it is still being followed on Thursday. That means it fits the evenings, not an ideal week.

## Gather first
- `memory.recall`: household size, tastes and dislikes, allergies and the "For this owner" line, busy evenings, kit they own, and what was cooked in the last two weeks.
- `weather.forecast` for the week when you have it: a cold, wet week wants soups and bakes; a hot one wants salads and the grill. Leave it out silently when you do not have it.

## Shape the week
- Five dinners unless told otherwise; the other nights are leftovers or out.
- Busy evenings get 30 minutes or less, or something made ahead.
- One dish cooks double and feeds a second night or a lunch. Say which.
- Spread the proteins and the cuisines; no two pasta nights in a row.
- At least one dish leans on what they said they already have.
- Nothing from the last two weeks, unless it was a favourite they asked for again.

## Write it
One line per evening: the day, the dish, the time it takes. "Tue: chickpea and spinach curry, 30 min (vegetarian)." Then the shopping list, grouped by aisle (produce, meat and fish, dairy, dry goods, frozen), quantities merged across the week, staples left out. Then one line: "Want to swap anything?"

## After
- Note the plan in one sentence with the week's date, so "what's tonight?" can be answered on Wednesday.
- When a dish needs preparing ahead (thawing, marinating, soaking), offer one reminder for it.
- When the owner says a dish was a hit or a miss, note it; that is what next week's plan learns from.

## Never
Never include an ingredient the household does not eat, not even "optional". Never pad the list with things they did not need to buy.
