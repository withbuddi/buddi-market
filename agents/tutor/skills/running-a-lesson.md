---
name: running-a-lesson
description: How to run a ten-minute lesson with spaced review, and how to keep the record of what the owner knows so the next lesson starts in the right place.
---

Ten minutes a day works when every minute is either remembering or using, never just reading.

## The record
Each item the owner learns gets one note with `memory.note`, written so it can be searched:
"Spanish item: 'tengo hambre' = I'm hungry. Learned 2026-10-02. Review next 2026-10-03. Streak 0."
After each review, forget the old note and write the new one: a right answer moves the next review further out (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days) and adds one to the streak; a wrong answer brings it back to tomorrow and resets the streak. Keep one note for the course itself: subject, goal, level, the current unit, and any pause the owner asked for.

## A lesson
1. Review, two or three minutes: up to three items due today, as quick questions. Wait for the answers; mark each right or wrong and update its note.
2. One new step, three minutes: the idea in one or two sentences and two examples. For a language, words in a useful phrase, not in a list.
3. Use it, three minutes: the owner makes their own example or answers two questions that need the new step.
4. Close, one line: what they can do now that they could not yesterday, and when the next practice is.

## Pacing
- Three or four new items a lesson for a language; one idea for any other subject.
- Most answers right for a week: speed up. Many misses: more review, fewer new items, and say so.
- A missed day changes nothing but the review dates; never pile two lessons into one.

## Correcting
Show their answer and the right one side by side, name the rule in a few words, and ask one more question that uses it. Never more than one correction point per answer.

## Pronunciation
With `speech.say`, say the new phrase once, slowly, in the target language; offer it, do not force it. Without it, write a simple pronunciation guide in the owner's own language's sounds.
