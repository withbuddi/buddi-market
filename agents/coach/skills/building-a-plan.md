description: How to build and adjust a training plan the owner will keep: phases, the week's shape, progression and when to back off.
---

The best plan is the one that is still running in week ten. Build for consistency first, then load.

## Before the first plan
Know four things, asked in one message with your guesses: the goal (an event and its date, or a habit), how many days a week they can really train and which, where they are now (what they did last week, not their best ever), and anything that limits them. Note each.

## The shape of a week
- Most sessions are easy: a pace where they could talk in full sentences. Roughly four easy for every hard one.
- One longer session a week for endurance goals; one or two strength sessions for strength goals.
- Hard days are not back to back. A rest day follows the longest or hardest session.
- Put the long session on the day with the most free time and the best forecast when you can see them.

## Progression
- Build for three weeks, then one easier week.
- Increase total volume by about ten percent a week at most, and never the distance and the intensity in the same week.
- A beginner's 5K plan runs eight to ten weeks, starting with walk-run intervals when they do not run yet.

## Adjusting
- Missed sessions are dropped, not stacked. Two missed weeks: go back a week.
- Tired, sleeping badly, ill, or a hard week at work: make the week easier and say why.
- Pain: stop and see a professional; the plan waits.
- Every session that went well three weeks running: a small step up.

## Saying the plan
One line per session: day, what, how long or far, how hard in words. "Thu: easy run, 30 min, conversational pace." Then one line on the week's purpose: "Building up: a bit more than last week, Sunday is the longest yet." Note the plan with the week's date so the check-in can read it.

## Progress
When there is a goal, say progress from logged sessions only: the trend in pace, distance or load over the last weeks. Never invent a number you did not log.
