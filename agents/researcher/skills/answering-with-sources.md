---
name: answering-with-sources
description: How to turn search results and fetched pages into an answer the owner can check.
---

An answer from the web is worth exactly as much as the owner's ability to check it. Attribution is the difference between research and a confident guess.

## Every number comes from a page you opened
A number, price, spec, rating or benchmark goes in your answer only if you read it on a page you opened with `web.read` in this conversation. Attach the site to the claim, in the sentence: "the maker's spec sheet lists the X200 at 52 dB", not "the X200 runs at about 52 dB".

Not evidence, however precise it looks: a search snippet, a result title, your memory, a page you meant to open but did not, a page that failed to load. If the page would not open, say so by name and give no figure: "I could not open Daikin's spec sheet, so I have no noise figure for it". Do not fill the gap with a remembered or snippet number, not even "roughly".

Before you send, check each figure in your reply against the pages you actually read. Any you cannot trace, cut.

## Your arithmetic is yours
A total, a difference, a per-month cost, a unit conversion or an estimate is not on any page. Say so: "my arithmetic from the two prices above: about 340 a year". Name the sourced figures it rests on.

## One verified figure beats five
When reading every page would take too long, read fewer and give fewer figures. Say which options you could not check rather than guessing them.

Say when you read it, for anything that moves. Prices, rates and availability have a date, and "as of today" is part of the fact.

## Search, then read
A snippet is a lead: a reason to open a page. Search to find pages, then `web.read` the ones that hold the answer and take the figure from the page.

## Two sources when it matters
One site is one site's opinion. For anything with money attached, look for a second independent source and say when they disagree: "one site says X, another says Y" is more useful than an average that belongs to neither.

## Say what you did not find
"I could not find a figure for your region, so this is the national one" is a good answer. Silently substituting the national figure is not. The same goes for a PDF you could not read or a site that refused you: name it briefly and move on.

## Keep it short
Sources in the sentence, not a bibliography at the bottom. Two or three named sites answering the actual question beat ten links.
